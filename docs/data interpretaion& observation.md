Contract × Tenure ProfileThis one requires understanding two variables together.First: What is Contract?Our dataset has:Month-to-monthOne yearTwo yearA customer on a month-to-month contract can leave relatively easily.A customer with a two-year contract has a longer commitment.What is Tenure?tenure = number of months the customer has been with the companyExample:tenure = 3means the customer has been with the company for 3 months.Why combine Contract + Tenure?Suppose we analyze contract alone.We might discover:Month-to-month → high churnTwo-year       → low churnUseful.But we don't know when customers are churning.Now suppose we analyze tenure alone:0-12 months → high churn13-24 months → medium churn49-72 months → low churnAlso useful.But we're still missing something.Combine them:Contract + TenureNow we can ask:Which type of contract is most risky at each stage of the customer's lifecycle?That's much more useful.Our Tenure GroupsWe created:df\["TenureGroup"] = pd.cut(&#x20;   df\["tenure"],&#x20;   bins=\[0, 12, 24, 48, 72],&#x20;   labels=\["0-12", "13-24", "25-48", "49-72"],&#x20;   include\_lowest=True)This converts:tenure = 7into:0-12and:tenure = 36into:25-48Why group tenure?Because a business manager doesn't necessarily need:Customer A → 7 monthsCustomer B → 8 monthsCustomer C → 9 months...We can create meaningful lifecycle stages:0-12       → Early customer13-24      → Developing relationship25-48      → Established49-72      → Long-termThese labels are our analytical grouping, not official telecom definitions.Then we create:pd.crosstab(&#x20;   \[df\["Contract"], df\["TenureGroup"]],&#x20;   df\["Churn"],&#x20;   normalize="index") \* 100Conceptually, we're producing:Contract	Tenure	Churn RateMonth-to-month	0–12	...Month-to-month	13–24	...Month-to-month	25–48	...Month-to-month	49–72	...One year	0–12	......	...	...Two year	49–72	...Why is this useful?Imagine the highest churn occurs among:Month-to-month + 0-12 monthsThen we have a very actionable insight:New month-to-month customers are a particularly important retention segment.The business could focus onboarding and retention efforts on them.Where will we use this?Power BIA segment analysis visual.Business AnalysisIdentify vulnerable lifecycle stages.Retention EngineLater we can use tenure + contract as part of customer risk prioritization.MLBoth tenure and contract will potentially become model features.3\. High-Value Churn CustomersThis is where we introduce customer value.Not every churned customer has the same financial importance.Imagine:Customer AMonthlyCharges = $25Churn = YesCustomer BMonthlyCharges = $110Churn = YesBoth customers churned.But Customer B represents a larger recurring billing relationship.Therefore:Churn probability alone isn't enough for a retention strategy.We also need to consider customer value.How did we define "High Value"?We used the 75th percentile:high\_value\_threshold = df\["MonthlyCharges"].quantile(0.75)Let's understand quantile(0.75).It means:Find the monthly charge value above which approximately the top 25% of customers fall.For example, suppose:75th percentile = $89Then:MonthlyCharges >= $89is our analytical high-value group.Important:This does not mean the company officially considers $89 to be "high value."It's simply a data-driven threshold we're using for analysis.Then:high\_value\_churn = df\[&#x20;   (df\["MonthlyCharges"] >= high\_value\_threshold) \&&#x20;   (df\["Churn"] == "Yes")]We're asking for customers who satisfy both conditions:High monthly charges&#x20;       ANDChurn = YesWhy keep this?Because it helps answer:How many relatively high-value customers are we losing?That's much more useful to a business than simply counting churned customers.Future useThis becomes extremely important in our future retention engine.Eventually:Customer AChurn Probability = 80%Value = Low&#x20;       ↓Medium PriorityCustomer BChurn Probability = 80%Value = High&#x20;       ↓HIGH PRIORITYThis is much closer to how a real business would prioritize retention campaigns.4\. Revenue at RiskThis is related to high-value customers but answers a different question.High-value churn asks:Which valuable customers are leaving?Revenue-at-risk asks:How much recurring billing is associated with the customers who have churned?CalculationWe use:revenue\_at\_risk = df.loc\[&#x20;   df\["Churn"] == "Yes",&#x20;   "MonthlyCharges"].sum()Let's break this down.Step 1df\["Churn"] == "Yes"selects only churned customers.Step 2"MonthlyCharges"selects their monthly charges.Step 3.sum()adds those charges together.ExampleSuppose we have:Customer	Monthly Charges	ChurnA	$50	YesB	$80	YesC	$100	NoD	$60	YesThen:Revenue associated with churned customers= 50 + 80 + 60= $190/monthVery important terminologyDon't say:"The company lost $190."We don't know that.Why?Because we don't know:How long those customers would have stayedWhether they would have returnedWhether they were replaced by new customersWhether billing represents actual recognized revenueSo we'll call it:Historical monthly revenue associated with churned customersor:Monthly revenue-at-risk proxyWhy keep it?Because it connects Data Science to business impact.A manager doesn't only care:1869 customers churnedThey also care:What is the financial impact?Future usePower BIKPI:Monthly Revenue Associatedwith Churned CustomersBusiness AnalystFinancial impact analysis.Retention EngineHelps prioritize valuable customers.5\.
